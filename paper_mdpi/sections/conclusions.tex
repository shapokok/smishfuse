\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We asked whether transformers are necessary for real-time smishing detection when the evaluation controls for duplicates, for obfuscation that the model has not seen and for the cost of inference. On three deduplicated public corpora, under a protocol frozen before the test data were read, with \val{n-seeds} seeds and paired tests with Holm correction, we compared two operating points of a calibrated character-level detector with classical, neural and transformer baselines, a few-shot language model and re-implemented published methods.

In our data, transformers were not needed for quality, and those trained without augmentation lost much of their quality under unseen obfuscation. The re-implemented published transformer detectors fell from between \val{sh-clean-min} and \val{sh-clean-max} to between \val{sh-ho20-min} and \val{sh-ho20-max} macro F1 under the strongest held-out obfuscation, while Lite, a calibrated linear model over character n-grams trained on clean messages, went from \val{svmchar-shim-clean} to \val{svmchar-shim-ho20}; these differences are significant by both tests. Lite had the highest clean macro F1 in the three-class task (\val{svmchar-clean-f1}) and needed \val{lat-svmchar-p50}~ms per message on one CPU thread, \val{speed-ratio-min} to \val{speed-ratio-max} times less than the published transformers. Full, the cascade that passes uncertain messages to DistilBERT, sent \val{lat-cascade-routed-pct}\% of the messages to the transformer and had the lowest error cost under strong obfuscation, but it did not differ significantly from Lite on clean data. A transformer stage is therefore an option for a gateway that wants a fallback on uncertain messages, not a requirement for accuracy. The treatment of duplicates changed cross-validated macro F1 by up to \val{infl-max-drop} points, so results on public SMS corpora should be reported with an explicit deduplication procedure and with significance tests.

The case study for Kazakhstan showed that models trained on English messages miss most fraudulent messages in Russian and Kazakh, that translation is too slow and lossy for real-time use, and that synthetic adaptation trades missed fraud for false alarms. Future work should evaluate adaptive attacks and paraphrasing, collect real labelled messages in regional languages, use thresholds that account for the cost of errors, and test a multilingual first stage.
